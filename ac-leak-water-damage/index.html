AC Leak Water Damage in Sandusky, OH

Air conditioners make water as they cool the air. When the drain path fails, that water ends up in your ceiling, closet or floor.

Call (567) 406-9153

Air conditioners remove humidity from the air, and that moisture has to go somewhere. AC leak water damage in Sandusky, OH happens when the path to the drain breaks down and the water spills inside. Unlike a burst pipe, this is usually a slow leak, but it can soak a ceiling or floor over days.

If you see water around an air handler, a stain near a vent or a wet closet floor, switch the system off at the thermostat and call. Keep the unit off until an HVAC technician checks the drain path.

How a cooling system ends up leaking

The coil collects condensation in a pan, and a drain line carries it away. Over time, algae and debris narrow the line. Once it clogs, water backs up, overflows the pan and runs into whatever sits below. In attic installations, the first sign is often a ceiling stain.

Why AC leaks are easy to miss

These leaks start small. A damp stain behind a closet door, a slightly soft floor near a vent or a musty odor in one room can all be early signs. Callers to restoration lines often describe a condensation line that has been backing up for some time before the damage was visible.

Because the water arrives slowly, drywall and insulation stay wet for long periods. That is the setting where mold gets started, so assess the area before painting or patching. EPA guidance says water-damaged materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and a slow leak can exceed that window long before you notice it.

What an assessment and drying visit includes

The crew traces the moisture with a meter and thermal camera, checks the ceiling and walls around the unit and looks at the flooring under any vents. If the area is wet, they set air movers and a dehumidifier and monitor readings until the structure is dry.

A ceiling stain from an attic unit follows the plan in ceiling water damage repair. Wall moisture follows wall and drywall repair, and a wet floor goes through hardwood floor water damage repair. All of it relies on the method in structural drying.

Before the crew visits

Turn the cooling system off until the drain path is checked. Photograph the stain and the wet area, and note when you first saw it. If a ceiling is sagging or dripping near a light fixture, stay clear and call the electric utility or 911 for any electrical concern.

For the drying that follows a condensate overflow, the published reference is ANSI/IICRC S500, and a company should be able to say whether its crews work to it.
