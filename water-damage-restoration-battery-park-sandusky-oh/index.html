Water Damage Restoration at Battery Park, Sandusky, OH

Cleanup and drying for waterfront condos, nearby homes and the mixed-use buildings planned for the Battery Park site.

Battery Park is the waterfront district at the eastern edge of downtown Sandusky, on Sandusky Bay. It is a place that is changing. The former Battery Park Marina site, described in local news as a long-underused parking lot, is being redeveloped as a mixed-use waterfront project. People still search for help here, and this page says plainly what is on the ground now and what is only planned.

What exists today and what is only planned

News reports in 2026 describe a roughly $229 million mixed-use project announced in 2023, with housing, a hotel, shops, restaurants, a waterfront park and a boardwalk. Phase one is underground site preparation, and the city says full development will take three to five years. A project master plan describes about 10 acres, around 240 condominiums and apartments, about 40,000 square feet of retail, a hotel and a private marina.

None of that is a promise of what will be built or when. Buildings that do not exist yet cannot leak, so for now the useful audience is the owners and tenants of nearby properties, plus the managers who will one day run the new ones. Real estate listings within a mile of the park show a mix of older single-family houses and small condo units with water views.

Waterfront and multi-unit water damage

In a multi-story building the water rarely stays where it started. A supply line in a top-floor unit, a failed ice-maker line or a washer hose can wet the units below through shared floors and ceilings. When you call, name the building, the floor and the unit that is wet, and say what you see below. That lets the crew plan for several spaces at once.

Cooling equipment is another source in condo and apartment buildings. A clogged condensate line can overflow into a ceiling, which is explained on the AC leak water damage page. Stains and sagging on the underside of a floor are covered under ceiling water damage repair.

Before anyone enters a wet unit

  1. Do not step into standing water if outlets, cords or a panel are wet.
  2. Ask the building manager or the property owner to cut power to the unit from the panel, from a dry position.
  3. Close the unit's water shutoff only if you can reach it safely, and tell the manager which valve it was.
  4. Keep people out of hallways and stairs where water is running, because floors can be slippery and ceilings can fail.
  5. If there is a gas smell or any smoke, leave and call 911 from outside.

Weather off the bay

Exposed shoreline buildings take the wind first. Storm-driven rain can find a failed seal at a window, a roof edge or a balcony door, and the water often travels along framing before it shows. The storm water damage cleanup page explains how a crew traces entry points and dries the cavities. How cleanup and drying work in an occupied building

A crew starts by extracting standing water, then maps moisture with meters and thermal imaging, and dries with air movers and dehumidifiers. In a condo, the plan also covers noise, hours and how the equipment is powered. The details of the first step are on the water extraction page.

Before work starts you hear the scope and how the price is set, including what depends on readings still to come. A crew keeps photographs, readings and equipment logs for your records, and you can pass them to your association and your insurer.

Local note for the Sandusky waterfront

The site sits across the bay from Cedar Point, and the master plan describes a future ferry link to a waterfront park. Redevelopment work is also subject to state review of shoreline construction, which was still pending in 2026. If you own or manage property near the park, check the latest from the City of Sandusky before assuming a schedule.

Back from the water, downtown Sandusky and the residential East Side have their own pages, and the service area page lists every town on the line.

Insurance and documentation

Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and your policy terms. Sudden pipe and appliance failures are often treated differently from gradual leaks and flood. Read your policy, ask your insurer and your association, and photograph the damage before anything is moved. No restoration company decides coverage for you.
