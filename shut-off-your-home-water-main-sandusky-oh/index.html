How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Sandusky, OH

Where the valve usually is, how to turn it, what to do when it will not budge and what stays your responsibility.

Call (567) 406-9153

When a pipe bursts, every minute the water runs makes the damage worse. The single most useful thing you can do is shut off the water at the main valve. Most people do not know where theirs is until they need it. This guide helps Sandusky, OH homeowners find the shutoff now, practice with it and understand what to do if it fails.

Where the main shutoff usually is

The main valve is located where the water line enters the house. In northern Ohio, that is often in the basement or crawl space on the wall nearest the street, close to the water meter. Look for a pipe coming through the foundation with a valve and a meter on it. In homes with slab foundations, it may be in a utility closet, a garage or near the water heater.

There are two kinds of valve. A gate valve has a round wheel that you turn clockwise to close. A ball valve has a lever, and the water is off when the lever sits across the pipe at a right angle. Ball valves are easier to use and less likely to stick.

Find it before you need it

  1. Walk the basement or utility area along the wall facing the street.
  2. Find the meter and follow the pipe back to the nearest valve.
  3. Tag the valve with a bright label and take a photo of it.
  4. Close it slowly, then open a faucet to confirm the water stops.
  5. Open the valve again and note which way it turned.

Practice once a year. Valves that never move can corrode and seize, which is the worst time to find out. If a valve is stiff, ask a plumber to replace it before an emergency.

Other shutoffs worth knowing

Main shutoffWhere the line enters the houseAll water in the building

Washing machine valvesBehind the machineThe hot and cold hoses

If a leak comes from one fixture, closing its own stop valve stops the water without shutting down the whole house. A leak from a washing machine or a dishwasher often has a valve right behind it. A leak from a water heater can be stopped at its cold inlet, as described on the water heater leak cleanup page.

What to do when the main will not close

Do not force it. Older gate valves can snap or leak around the stem when pushed hard. Try turning back and forth a little to free it. If it still will not move, look for the meter valve, which belongs to the water provider, and call them for help. For a major break, call your water provider's emergency line and describe the situation.

Electrical safety around a flooded basement

Do not walk into water to reach a valve if the water is near outlets, cords, the panel or a sump pump. Cut power only from a dry position. If you cannot reach a shutoff safely, call the water provider, the electric utility or 911 first. A valve is not worth an electric shock.

After the water is off

Once the flow stops, open the lowest faucet to drain remaining water from the lines, and keep clear of any electrical risk. Photograph the damage and call the 24/7 line. Fast removal of standing water is covered on the water extraction page, and the repair sequence for a split line is described on the burst pipe page.

Remember that a plumber repairs the pipe, and a restoration crew removes the water and dries the structure. You will usually need both. Having the main off before the crew arrives makes their work much easier, because they can start extraction without more water coming in.

Local note for Sandusky and Huron

The City of Sandusky operates its own water system, and the public line up to the meter is the city's responsibility. Inside the house, the main valve and piping are yours. If you are unsure where the city's responsibility ends and yours begins, contact the city's water department before an emergency.

Huron is served by its own city utility, so confirm which provider handles your address. Calls from either town reach the same dispatch line, as described on the service area page and the Huron page.

A winter note

Cold weather makes the main valve matter more. If you leave a Sandusky home empty during a freeze, shutting the main and draining the lines reduces the chance of a burst. Ask a plumber how to do it safely for your system, particularly if you have a boiler, a sprinkler system or a water softener. The causes of winter pipe failures are covered on the frozen pipe page.

Take ten minutes this week to find your valve, label it and test it. It costs nothing, and it is the most useful prevention step there is.

Questions about the main shutoff

Look where the water line enters the building, often in a basement, a crawl space or a utility room, near the meter or the water heater. In some homes it is in a garage or an exterior box.

A wheel valve closes clockwise. A lever valve closes when the handle sits at a right angle to the pipe. Turn it slowly, and do not force a stuck valve.

Do not use a pipe wrench or heavy force, which can break it. Try the curb stop at the meter if you can reach it safely, or call the water provider. Then call a plumber to replace the valve.

Generally the water provider owns the line to the meter, and the homeowner owns the piping and valve inside the property. Check with your provider if you are unsure.
