Kitchen Water Damage in Sandusky, OH

Kitchens have more water lines than any other room. When a supply line or drain fails, cabinets and floors take the damage first.

Call (567) 406-9153

The kitchen holds the water supply for the sink, the dishwasher, the refrigerator and sometimes the dishwasher and disposal drains, all within a few feet of wood cabinets. Kitchen water damage restoration in Sandusky, OH deals with what happens when one of those lines fails and the cabinets and floor soak up the water.

If water is under the sink, close the two shutoff valves under it. If you cannot find them, shut the main water valve. Take everything out of the cabinet, wipe up water and photograph the wet areas before the crew arrives.

Under the sink: the hiding place for kitchen leaks

Most kitchen leaks start under the sink or behind the dishwasher. Water runs through the cabinet floor, down into the subfloor and sideways along toe kicks. The first sign is usually a swollen cabinet base, a soft floor or a musty smell, long after the leak began.

Cabinets: save, dry or replace

Cabinet material matters. Solid wood and plywood boxes with intact finishes can often be dried in place. Particleboard and MDF absorb water, swell and lose their strength, so they are usually replaced. Laminate and countertops can lift if water gets under the edges.

Cabinet drying uses small drying equipment and sometimes requires removing toe kicks or drilling small holes to push air into the cavity. The crew will tell you what can be kept before any cabinet is removed.

Floors and walls around the kitchen

Kitchen flooring may be tile, vinyl, laminate or wood. Each reacts differently, and water often goes under the surface layer. For wood, see hardwood floor water damage repair. For wet drywall, the wall and drywall page covers the repair.

If the source was a supply line, the burst pipe page explains the failure and the appliance leak page covers dishwasher and refrigerator lines. The drying process follows structural drying.

Quick checks before the crew arrives

  • Close the shutoff valves under the sink, or the main water valve.
  • Unplug the dishwasher or refrigerator only if the floor is dry.
  • Empty the cabinet and photograph the damage.
  • Keep the failed part if you can, since your insurer may want to see it.

The published reference for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500. Ask whoever dries your cabinets whether the crew follows it, and how moisture in the toe kicks and subfloor will be checked.

Electrical safety around a wet kitchen

Water near the stove, dishwasher, refrigerator or outlets is a shock risk. Cut power only if you can reach the panel from dry ground.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 from outside before anything else.

A sudden supply line failure is often covered by homeowners insurance, while long-term seepage is commonly excluded. Check your policy and ask what documentation they need.

Kitchen water damage questions from Sandusky homeowners

Solid wood and well-sealed cabinets can sometimes be dried and kept. Particleboard and MDF swell when wet and often have to be replaced. A moisture reading on the cabinet base decides.

Dry the cabinet base, then check supply lines, shutoff valves, the drain trap and the garbage disposal with a paper towel. Look for a drip, wet wood or a mineral crust at connections.

The failed appliance line is the appliance leak. The cabinets, floor and walls it wet are the kitchen job. Many real losses involve both.

Sudden failures are often covered, including water from a burst supply line. Slow leaks that went unfixed are often excluded. Ask your insurer and keep photos of the source.

It depends on what was wet and how long drying takes. A kitchen can often stay partly usable during drying, and the crew can tell you what will be off-limits.
