Water Damage Categories Explained for Sandusky, OH Homeowners

Category 1, 2 and 3 water, plus the classes of damage, and why the category changes what a crew does.

Call (567) 406-9153

When a crew arrives, one of the first things they decide is what kind of water they are dealing with. That choice drives safety gear, what gets thrown away, what gets dried and how long the job runs. The published industry reference is ANSI/IICRC S500, and it defines three categories of water and four classes of damage. This guide explains them in plain words for Sandusky, OH homeowners.

Category 1: clean water

Category 1 water comes from a sanitary source and poses little risk at the moment it is released. Typical examples are a burst supply line, a leaking faucet, a failed water heater tank or an overflowing sink with clean water. Porous materials such as carpet and drywall can often be dried in place if the response is quick.

Category 2: gray water

Category 2 water has some contamination and can cause illness if people are exposed. Typical sources are washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow with urine but no feces, sump pump overflow and aged water from Category 1 that has started to deteriorate. Porous materials such as carpet pad are often removed, and hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized.

Category 3: black water

Category 3 water is grossly contaminated and may contain pathogens. Sewage backups, toilet overflow with feces, floodwater from rivers or the lake and standing water that has supported growth belong here. Porous materials that touched it are generally discarded, and the structure is cleaned and disinfected with protective gear. The detailed steps are on the sewage backup cleanup page and the flood damage cleanup page.

The three categories at a glance
CategoryTypical sourcesWhat usually happens to porous materials
Category 1Supply line, tank, faucetOften dried in place if handled quickly
Category 2Washer or dishwasher discharge, aged supply waterPad and insulation removed, rest cleaned and dried
Category 3Sewage, floodwater, surface waterRemoved and discarded, structure disinfected

Do not memorize the list. The point is that you can describe the source to a dispatcher, and the dispatcher helps you work out the category.

Water changes category as time passes

A clean water leak does not stay clean. Bacteria multiply in standing water, and materials add contaminants. A supply line break that sat for several days can behave like Category 2, particularly in warm weather or in a damp basement. This is one more reason the response window matters, alongside the EPA's guidance that water-damaged areas should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.

Classes of damage: how much water the materials hold

Class describes the evaporation challenge, which is how much water was absorbed and how easily it will leave. S500 sorts losses into four classes.

Classes of water damage
ClassWhat it meansExample
Class 1Slowest evaporation load, least absorptionA small area with low-permeance materials
Class 2Large area, water wicked up walls less than 24 inchesA room of wet carpet and wet lower drywall
Class 3Water came from above, saturated most surfacesCeiling, walls, insulation and carpet all wet
Class 4Deep saturation in dense materials needing special dryingHardwood, plaster, concrete or brick

Class and category are independent. A Class 2 loss can be Category 1 or Category 3. A crew needs both facts to plan drying and cleaning. Dense materials such as hardwood and concrete are why some jobs take longer, as described on the structural drying page.

When you cannot tell what the water is

Treat unknown water as contaminated until a professional says otherwise. Wear boots and gloves, avoid skin contact, keep children and pets out and wash well afterward. Never mix cleaning products, and keep away from water near electrical equipment.

How a crew determines the category

The crew asks where the water came from, how long it has been there and what it has touched. They look at the water's color, smell and the materials involved. Test results or lab work are not usually needed for standard losses, but they may be used for unusual ones. The decision is documented so the plan and the safety steps can be explained to you.

You can help by describing the source accurately and not cleaning anything before the crew arrives. If you have already mopped, tell the dispatcher what you did and what you used.

Local note for Sandusky and Huron

In Sandusky and Huron, the most common surprises are basement water and drain backups. Heavy rain can overwhelm combined or older drains, and low-lying properties near the bay, river or lake may take on surface water. That water is generally treated as Category 3. Look up your address on the FEMA flood map and ask the City of Sandusky building division or the City of Huron about drainage conditions on your street.

Why the category matters to your wallet and your health

Higher categories mean more protective gear, more materials removed and more disinfection, which raises cost. They also change what is safe to keep. Clean-water carpet might be dried and saved. Black-water carpet is never saved. If your homeowners policy excludes flood or sewer backup, that exclusion usually applies to Category 3 events. The water extraction page describes the first step for any category.
