Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Sandusky, OH

A building can look dry and still hold water. Structural drying finds the hidden moisture and dries it with measured equipment and daily readings.

Call (567) 406-9153

Structural drying and dehumidification in Sandusky, OH is the stage that decides whether a water event ends with a dry, sound building or a hidden moisture problem. Extraction removes the standing water, but wall cavities, subfloors, insulation and framing can still be wet. This page explains how that moisture is found and removed.

Walls still damp days after the leak?

If your house smells musty or the baseboards feel cool and damp a few days after a leak, water is still in the structure. That is the situation structural drying is built for. The trade name for the process is water mitigation, and it relies on measured readings, not guesses.

Moisture mapping: measuring before drying

The process starts with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera. Readings are taken on drywall, baseboards, framing and flooring, then recorded room by room. The map shows how far water traveled and gives a baseline to measure drying progress.

Drying goals are set by comparing wet readings against dry reference readings from unaffected areas of the same home. Work continues until the wet areas match the dry ones.

Water mitigation: the equipment and what it does

Drying equipment in plain words
EquipmentWhat it doesWhere it is used
Air moversPush air across wet surfaces to speed evaporationFloors, wall bases, carpet, cabinet bases
LGR dehumidifiersPull moisture out of the air so it can keep evaporatingWhole rooms and closed structures
Desiccant dehumidifiersAbsorb humidity in cold or very damp conditionsCold spaces, crawl spaces
Wall cavity drying systemsMove dry air into a wall or cabinet baseClosed cavities that cannot be opened
Moisture meters and thermal imagingMeasure and map moistureEvery stage, from start to finish

The industry reference for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration. You can ask any company whether its crews follow it.

Daily monitoring and when drying is done

Drying is a process, not a single visit. Technicians return to check readings, move equipment and adjust the plan. When readings in wet materials match the dry reference, equipment is removed and rebuilding can start.

Drying works best when water is removed promptly. EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, which is why water extraction and drying follow quickly after a loss.

Where drying fits in each kind of loss

Every water event ends with drying, but each source has quirks. Flooring is the focus of hardwood floor water damage repair and carpet water damage restoration. Walls are covered in wall and drywall repair.

A basement or a crawl space has its own humidity profile. The basement water damage and crawl space pages describe how drying adapts there.
