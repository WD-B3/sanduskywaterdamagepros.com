Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Sandusky, OH

Wood absorbs water and moves as it dries. The first days decide whether planks flatten again or have to be replaced.

Call (567) 406-9153

Wood floors change shape when they take on water. Hardwood floor water damage repair in Sandusky, OH starts with the same question after every leak: can the planks dry flat, or have they moved too far? The answer depends on how fast the water was removed and how the drying is managed.

If your wood floor got wet, take up rugs and furniture legs that hold moisture against the planks, wipe the surface and call. Do not seal the floor with wax or polish, and do not run a heater near it, which can dry the top faster than the bottom.

Where floor water comes from

The usual culprits are a failed washing machine valve, a dishwasher or refrigerator line, a leaking HVAC condensate line, a plumbing leak under the floor and storm water entering at a door. Some callers describe a walnut floor that began to cup after a condensation line backed up for some time.

Because the source changes the repair, we check it early. Appliance failures are covered in appliance leak water damage, and cooling line leaks in AC leak water damage.

How wood floors are dried

The crew removes standing water, then measures the planks and the subfloor with a moisture meter. Specialized floor drying equipment pulls moisture out of the boards while air movers and dehumidifiers control the room. Some drying systems use mats or small vents to dry under the surface.

Drying wood takes patience. Pushing it too hard can lead to splitting or crowning. The goal is to bring the planks back to the same moisture level as dry wood in the rest of the house, following the approach in structural drying and dehumidification.

When to dry, when to sand and when to replace

Once the wood is dry and stable, a mild cup can be corrected by sanding and refinishing. Buckled or delaminated planks, or boards that stayed wet long enough to darken or rot, are usually replaced. Matching new boards to old ones is a finish decision as much as a repair one.

Remember that water may be in the subfloor and underlayment too. If the floor needs to come up, the crew will tell you before it does. A wet wall base that wicked upward is addressed through wall and drywall repair.

Hardwood is a dense material, and ANSI/IICRC S500 treats drying it as its own challenge. Ask any company whether its crews work to that published standard and how it will log readings on your boards.

Slip and electrical safety on wet floors

Wet wood is slick, so wear shoes with grip and keep children and pets out of the room.

Do not run power strips or portable heaters across a wet floor, and cut power only from a dry position.
