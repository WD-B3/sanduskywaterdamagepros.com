How to Prevent Water Damage While You Are Away From Your Sandusky, OH Home

A checklist for vacations, winter trips and seasonal homes, from shutoffs to monitors to who has a key.

Call (567) 406-9153

The worst water damage stories are about homes that were empty. A hose lets go on a Tuesday, no one is home and the water runs until the weekend. By then it has soaked floors, ceilings and everything below. Whether you are leaving for a week or for the winter, a short checklist can prevent that. This guide is written for Sandusky, OH homeowners, with a focus on cold weather and seasonal lakeshore homes.

Before you leave

  1. Shut off the main water valve if you will be gone more than a few days.
  2. Open a low faucet to drain remaining water if you are shutting the main off for the winter.
  3. Turn the water heater to vacation mode or off, following its manual.
  4. Close the washing machine hose valves, which can fail under pressure.
  5. Check under sinks and around the water heater for any drips before you go.
  6. Set the thermostat to a steady temperature and do not turn it off in cold months.
  7. Tell a trusted person that you are away.

If you do not know where your shutoff is, see the steps for finding and testing it in our guide on shutting off your main. A valve you can find in the dark is worth more than any gadget.

Heat and pipes in cold months

Frozen pipes are the main winter risk for empty homes. Water that freezes expands, and a split line can run as soon as it thaws. Keep the heat on at a steady setting, open cabinet doors on exterior walls and insulate exposed pipes in unheated areas like crawl spaces and garages. A drip from a faucet on an exposed line can keep water moving, though it is not a sure fix. The details are on the frozen pipe water damage page.

If the home will be empty for a long season, draining the system is a stronger option. A plumber can help drain the lines, add antifreeze to traps and protect fixtures. Do this before the first hard freeze, not after.

Sensors and shutoffs

Small battery sensors cost little and sound an alarm or send a phone alert when they touch water. Put them where leaks are common: beside the water heater, behind the washing machine, under kitchen and bathroom sinks and near the sump pit. Automatic shutoff valves go further by closing the main when a sensor trips. Whatever you choose, test it before you leave.

Remember that a power outage can disable heat, sensors and sump pumps at the same time. A battery backup for the sump pump is worth considering if your home has one. The causes of basement flooding are covered on the basement water damage page.

If you return to a flooded home

Do not walk into standing water until you know the power is off to the area. If water touches outlets, cords or the panel, stay out and call the electric utility or 911. Close the main valve only from a dry position, photograph everything and then call the 24/7 line.

Name someone to check

The best protection is a person. Ask a neighbor, a relative or a property manager to walk through every few days. Give them a short written list: where the main valve is, where the thermostat is, what a leak might look like and who to call. Give them the dispatch number for water damage as well as your insurer's claim line.

Seasonal and lakeshore homes

Cottages, second homes and rentals face extra risk because they sit empty for long stretches. Fall closing should include draining the lines, closing valves, protecting traps and unplugging appliances. Spring opening should include opening the main slowly and checking every fixture for drips before leaving it alone.

Local note for Sandusky and Huron

The shoreline around Sandusky, Huron and the Lake Erie islands has many seasonal homes. Island and lakeshore properties can be harder to reach quickly, because ferry schedules and travel conditions affect how fast a crew gets there. Mention where you are when you call, and the dispatcher explains what is possible. Plan checks accordingly, and do not rely on a quick visit if no one is nearby.

If something goes wrong anyway

If you learn that water has been running, close the main valve if you can, have your checker go in only if it is safe and call the 24/7 line. Fast water extraction limits how far the damage spreads, and measured drying follows. Keep photographs and receipts for your insurer. A few minutes of setup before a trip can save weeks of repair after it.

Questions about leaving a home empty

It is a smart precaution. Closing the main valve removes pressure from the system, so a failed hose or pipe cannot run for days. Check with a plumber if you have a boiler, a sprinkler system or a water softener.

Keep the heat on and set to a steady temperature, and many guides suggest not going below about 55 degrees. Open cabinet doors on exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes.

They can help. Battery sensors alert you when water appears, and smart shutoff valves can close the main automatically. They need power or batteries and a way to notify you.

Pick someone you trust to walk through every few days, look for water and check the heat. Give them the shutoff location and the dispatch number.
